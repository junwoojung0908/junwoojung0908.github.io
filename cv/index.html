Research Experience

  • 2025.09–present

    Daejeon, Korea

    Research Intern
    Quantum Computing Lab (Prof. Jaewook Ahn), KAIST
    • Developed an approximation-ratio-resolved shots-to-solution (STS) framework for hybrid neutral-atom optimization, modeling postprocessed outputs via a degeneracy-weighted Boltzmann shell distribution parameterized by a single quality parameter β̃.
    • Introduced an excitation-matched random baseline β̃_rand(p_exc) to isolate genuine quantum structural exploitation from excitation-density effects, correcting a systematic confound in existing Rydberg benchmarking literature.
    • Ran QuEra Aquila experiments (King's-lattice MIS, N ∈ [30, 125], 500 shots/instance) and measured Δβ̃_ann = 0.23–0.40 across all system sizes and hardness bins, confirming that adiabatic annealing genuinely exploits low-energy solution structure.
    • Characterized the two-regime STS scaling: exponential in N for near-exact targets (r → 1), O(1) for relaxed targets, establishing the approximation-ratio dependence of the quantum advantage.
    • Built a deterministic error-mitigation (DEM) pipeline for Rydberg MIS using a binomial Hamming-shell noise model and entropy-controlled classical baselines (2^{N H₂(p)} scaling); analyzed Pasqal Fresnel datasets and extracted instance-dependent success scaling.
