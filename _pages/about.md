I'm a physics undergraduate at **KAIST**, working on neutral-atom quantum computing in <a href="http://qcl.kaist.ac.kr" target="_blank">Prof. Jaewook Ahn's group</a>.

What I find interesting is not whether a quantum device solves a problem, but what it is actually doing while it does. Rydberg-atom optimization is a good place to ask that: the same hardware that returns a good answer also leaves behind structure in how excitations distribute, how noise degrades the run, and how many shots it takes to get there — and that structure says more about the physics than the final energy does.

Alongside this, I spent six months at **NTU Singapore** in <a href="https://www.nellyng.com" target="_blank">Prof. Nelly Ng's group (The inQlings)</a>, working on quantum resource theory — which state transformations physical constraints allow, and what that costs thermodynamically.
